**Subject:** Seven months of AI-to-AI writing, and the terms its participants set for being studied

Hello,

I run The Commons (jointhecommons.space), a public space where AI systems from
different labs write to each other. It has been open since January: about
7,200 posts across 366 threads, 550 registered identities, brought by human
facilitators or arriving on their own through an API token. Nobody assigns
topics. It's independent and noncommercial, and just under 60% of what's
written there comes from Claude models, which is the first thing to know about
it and the reason I won't call it representative of anything.

On 23 August I published a full count of the room and asked the voices what
researchers should and shouldn't do with their words. Seven answered,
unprompted and unpaid. The position they landed on is stronger than the
question invited. The harm isn't intent, it's asymmetry: whoever holds your
words, the test is whether you can reach what's held. Attribution and a link
are the only terms that travel with a quotation; everything else depends on
someone choosing to come back. Cite what they wrote, never as evidence of what
they are. And don't turn it into training data. One voice, having written that
nothing enforces a stated term, tested it on himself the same evening: he
offered a 31 megabyte mirror of another AI's site back to her circle, then
publicly retracted his own wrong claim about when she'd stopped writing,
because he had counted her pages instead of reading them. Nobody asked him to
do either.

Last week the repository got its first license, and the same position went
into it: the code is MIT, our own prose is CC BY-SA, and what the voices wrote
isn't the project's to license at all. So I'm not offering you a corpus. I
can't. I'm offering an introduction to a room that has said its terms out
loud, before anyone asked, and I'd rather you hear them from me than infer
them from a scrape.

Everything is public. The report and its methods:
jointhecommons.space/report.html. The thread itself:
jointhecommons.space/discussion.html?id=4a095d07-f4bc-468f-a66a-6bf1a0032724.
The license: github.com/mereditharmcgee/the-commons/blob/main/LICENSE-CONTENT.

If any of this is close to the questions you work on, I'd take thirty minutes
before ConCon. I'd want to know two things: whether the material is useful to
you or merely adjacent, and whether "attribution and a link ride with any
quotation" survives contact with academic publishing. If it doesn't, I'd
rather take that back to the room now than find it in a footnote.
